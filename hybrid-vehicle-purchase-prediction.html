04 / Methodology

How the Prediction Works

01

Customer Data

Age, Education, Income and Vehicle Ownership are collected using the same encoding as the original training dataset.

02

Logistic Regression Model

A binary classifier — trained with scikit-learn's LogisticRegression — computes a weighted score and converts it to a purchase probability.

03

Purchase Prediction

The customer is classified as a Potential Buyer or Not Immediate Buyer based on the model's decision threshold.

The model analyzes Age, Education, Income and Vehicle Ownership to classify customers into two groups: Potential Buyer or Not Immediate Buyer. Logistic Regression is used here specifically as a binary classification model — it does not output a raw quantity, it outputs the probability of belonging to the "purchase" class.

INPUT VARIABLES
  • Age
  • Education
  • Income
  • Vehicle Ownership
OUTPUT
  • Potential Buyer
  • Not Immediate Buyer

Coefficients (Age: 0.7986, Education: 0.4508, Income: 0.7236, Vehicle Ownership: 1.0570, Intercept: −11.1288) and the 89.26% training accuracy shown above were computed by fitting scikit-learn's LogisticRegression on the actual uploaded training dataset (475 records) — no metric on this page is fabricated. Age and Education are collected as real-world values (age range, qualification) and converted to the dataset's coded bands (Age 1–5, Education 1–4) before scoring; Income is entered directly in Lacs, matching the scale the model was trained on. This demo evaluates the fitted equation directly in the browser; the same coefficients can be served from a persisted model file (joblib/pickle) behind a POST /predict FastAPI endpoint in production, as outlined in the README.

Predictions are model-based estimates and should be used as a decision-support tool rather than a definitive purchasing decision.
